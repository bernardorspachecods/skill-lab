# Audit

## Question

How should an LLM structure a web-research deliverable for its intended
consumer, and when should the answer, confidence, sources, and detailed audit
trail be presented separately?

## Claims and evidence

- C1 — The answer structure should reflect the user's requested use and
  organize findings in a way the consumer can act on or absorb. Seek evidence
  and distinguish usability research from workflow judgment.
- C2 — Putting direct findings before detailed process records can improve
  access to the answer while preserving auditability through references or
  separate artifacts. Verify the basis and limits.
- C3 — Confidence and sources may be better exposed at the point of use or in
  linked support depending on the task. Identify trade-offs and avoid a single
  required template for all outputs.

All claims are pending. Treat the user's example (practices grouped by topic or
situation with confidence and sources available separately) as a design
requirement for a representative case, not a universal format.

## Sources

Use relevant research on information presentation or human-AI interaction,
authoritative guidance on provenance and evidence reporting, and concrete
research-agent output evaluations where available. Record audience, task, and
limitations.

## Conflicts and limitations

Output preferences vary by consumer and task. Separating detail can hide
important uncertainty if the main answer lacks sufficient context. Balance
readability with traceability and disclose material caveats in the answer.

## Validation

Check that the recommended format keeps the answer useful on its own, exposes
material uncertainty, and leaves a direct path to supporting evidence without
reproducing the full audit.
