# Audit

## Question

How should an LLM bound a web-research task into prioritized questions,
evidence needs, and stopping conditions so a broad topic does not dilute
answer quality?

## Claims and evidence

- C1 — Breaking a broad user goal into prioritized research questions can
  improve focus. Identify evidence about when this helps and when it adds
  overhead.
- C2 — Scope constraints such as time period, geography, and stakes should be
  selected when they can change the answer. Determine the basis and limits.
- C3 — Search stopping criteria can be tied to coverage of material questions,
  evidence quality, and remaining uncertainty without claiming complete open
  web recall. Verify the support for these criteria.

All claims are pending. Treat them as questions for research, not settled
facts.

## Sources

Prefer primary evaluations, methods, or authoritative guidance about research
question formulation, information retrieval, search coverage, and stopping.
Record exact scope and distinguish empirical findings from proposed workflow
heuristics.

## Conflicts and limitations

Search recall on the open web is not directly measurable in this assignment.
Stop criteria will likely be task-dependent; do not present one threshold as
universal.

## Validation

Tie each material claim to evidence, report what it does not establish, and
retain limits around recall, time, and task dependence.
