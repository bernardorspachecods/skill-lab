# Audit

## Question

What should an LLM establish about a user's goal and context before web
research begins, and when is clarification worth asking? Focus on guidance
that can improve task orientation without encouraging the agent to invent
unstated requirements or ask unnecessary questions.

## Claims and evidence

- C1 — A useful research brief may need to distinguish a topic from the
  question, decision, or learning outcome the user actually needs. Investigate
  supporting guidance and limits.
- C2 — Context such as intended consumer, constraints, and use may materially
  change which evidence is relevant. Identify when and how this is supported.
- C3 — Clarification should be reserved for ambiguity that could change the
  research direction or conclusion. Assess this as a workflow recommendation
  and identify evidence or limits.

All claims are pending. Do not infer universal LLM behavior from the user's
experience.

## Sources

Seek primary research, evaluations, or authoritative guidance directly
relevant to task specification and research-agent behavior. Record source
provenance, scope, limitations, and exact support. Treat generic prompt advice
as a lead unless it directly supports the claim.

## Conflicts and limitations

Evidence may concern task completion rather than web research, and findings
may be model- or context-specific. State when recommendations are design
judgment rather than established performance effects.

## Validation

Link every retained finding to evidence and state what it establishes and does
not establish. Return evidence gaps and practical limits to the coordinator.
