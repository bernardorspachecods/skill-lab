# Assessment

## Conclusions

**Result: revision required before the SP-01 output is accepted.** The audit is
well structured and its mechanism map is evidence-linked, appropriately marked
as synthesis, and careful not to imply a universal taxonomy or efficacy. Its
source ledger usefully keeps source quality, fit to a particular claim, and
independence/overlap as separate judgments. It also records meaningful
terminology disagreements, failure cases, and limitations.

The unresolved issue is the coverage/stop account. The audit and working log
explicitly leave robotics/control, scientific workflows, labeling operations,
online experiments, AIOps, and AI software engineering vocabularies
unsearched or incompletely searched (audit, “Conflicts and limitations”;
working log, “Candidate source pool and dispositions”). These are plausible
direct-AI-information domains, and some may expose mechanism variants not
clearly represented by the current families—for example, state/action control,
experiment-design/data/model cycles, adaptive experiments, operational
incident/remediation cycles, or test-and-repair cycles. This does not establish
that any should become a new category; it means the claim that more searching
is unlikely to change the broad map is not yet demonstrated for the stated
breadth of SP-01.

## Evidence

- **Scope and distinction quality — substantially met.** The question and
  boundary are explicit (audit, “Question and boundary”); the map compares
  repeat unit, observed state/content, signal, changed object, objective,
  autonomy, and human role, and labels these dimensions as researcher
  synthesis (audit, “Candidate mechanism map,” C10). It includes generation,
  tool/environment interaction, human-led learning, preference alignment,
  retrieval, recommenders, deployment monitoring, and governance. This
  satisfies the requirement to distinguish cycles by mechanism rather than
  label alone.
- **Evidence and source handling — met for the mapped examples.** C1–C10 link
  to E entries; entries identify source locations and specify what each source
  does and does not establish. The ledger separately assesses provenance and
  source role/quality, claim relevance, and independence/overlap (audit,
  “Evidence entries” and “Sources”). In particular, it does not treat NIST's
  framework and report as independent corroboration (S9–S10), nor vendor
  pattern documentation as efficacy evidence (S8, S11, S13). The audit also
  marks S12's abstract-only inspection and S10's report-summary limitation.
- **Disputes and limitations — met.** The audit reports conflicting or
  unstable human-loop taxonomies, explains why lifecycle, architecture, and
  learning taxonomies cannot be collapsed, and includes counterexamples to
  “feedback always improves quality” (audit, “Conflicts and limitations” and
  “Validation”). Its caveats on proxy signals, autonomy, and source roles are
  material to the map.
- **Coverage/stopping rationale — not yet sufficiently supported.** Q1–Q5
  record exact searches and useful vocabulary expansion, while the stop note
  identifies the distinctions those paths surfaced (working log,
  “Search coverage / stopping notes”; audit, “Validation”). But the listed
  specialist gaps are only named, not tested for whether they reveal a
  materially different cycle or term family. Because the assignment asks for a
  broad candidate map and explicitly treats seed terms as leads rather than a
  closed list, this is a gap in the basis for stopping, not a requirement for
  exhaustive recall.

## Uncertainties

I have not run a fresh literature search, so I cannot determine whether the
specialized vocabularies would add a new mechanism family or only examples that
fit the existing dimensions. The concern is strongest for online/adaptive
experimentation, AIOps, scientific discovery workflows, and AI software
engineering, where the feedback object or operational unit may differ. Robotics
and control may reasonably remain adjacent unless the searched sources connect
the loop directly to AI information handling. Labeling operations may already
be adequately covered by active learning, interactive ML, and machine teaching.
These distinctions should be checked with a bounded coverage probe rather than
assumed either way.

## Recommendations

### Required correction

Add a bounded, documented coverage probe for the named specialist vocabularies
(robotics/control, scientific workflows, labeling operations, online
experiments, AIOps, and AI software engineering). The probe need not be a
systematic review or a deep survey: use targeted terminology/source-role
queries and inspect enough credible records to classify each area as (a) a
distinct mechanism/term family to add, (b) an instance already represented by
the current map, or (c) outside the direct-AI-information boundary, with a
short evidence-based reason. Update the working log and, if the probe changes
the map or boundary, the audit's claims/evidence and limitations. Then revise
the stopping rationale to explain what this probe establishes and what remains
uncertain. Do not claim exhaustive coverage.

### Optional advice

1. If practical after the required probe, inspect the full AAAI “Preposition
   Salad” paper and the full NIST AI 800-4 report. The current audit clearly
   discloses that only an abstract and report summary were inspected; these
   follow-ups could sharpen oversight and monitoring terminology but are not
   necessary to support the narrower statements presently made.

## Review limits

This is an audit-quality and scope-coverage review against the SP-01 criteria,
not a fresh systematic search or verification of every linked source passage.
The concern about omitted domains is a bounded coverage concern; whether any
would materially alter the map remains unresolved pending the required probe.
