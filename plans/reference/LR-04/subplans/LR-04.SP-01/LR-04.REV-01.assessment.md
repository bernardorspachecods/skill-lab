# Independent Review Assessment

## Overall assessment

The audit meets the assigned scope and completion criteria. Its central
conclusion is calibrated to the evidence: the located literature supports
context-dependent effects from emotional/importance-adjacent prompt additions,
but does not establish a reliable general performance benefit from bare
importance or priority wording. The source records are primary studies, the
main claims are traceable to sections, figures, tables, or supplementary
figures, and the audit distinguishes task correctness from subjective ratings
and human-behavior similarity. I found no required corrections before
coordinator reconciliation.

## Findings against criteria

### Scope and construct separation — meets

The audit defines its target as generic verbal priority/importance framing and
explicitly treats “This is very important to my career” as overlap evidence
because it conveys personal stakes (Question and scope; E1; Thematic synthesis
¶1). This is consistent with SP-01's distinction between generic priority
cues and consequences. It also recognizes that no located study cleanly
isolates a bare importance/high-priority label from stakes, emotion, added
instructions, and task content (Conflicts, limitations, and uncertainty,
final bullet). That qualification prevents the EmotionPrompt findings from
being overclaimed as a direct test of the narrow construct.

### Effects and outcome interpretation — meets

The audit represents positive, null/mixed, and adverse evidence. For E1, the
source confirms that the 8.00% Instruction Induction and 115% BIG-Bench
figures concern the average over 11 stimuli, and that “max” selects the best
stimulus; it also reports EP02's relative strength on Instruction Induction
and weakness on BIG-Bench (Li et al. 2023, §2.2/Table 1 and §3.3/Fig. 10;
[primary text](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.11760), especially lines 113–155,
316–319). The audit correctly flags the near-zero BIG-Bench baseline as a
reason not to interpret 115% as a percentage-point gain.

The same-team expansion is not counted as independent corroboration. It is
used for the mixed benchmark pattern: EP02 performs poorly on BIG-Bench-Hard
(Li et al. 2024, §4.3/Fig. 8(b); [PMLR paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/li24bs.html);
[primary text](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.11111), lines 194–196). The combined
GPT-4V result is correctly labeled as multimodal and not as a clean text-only
test (Table 2; lines 207–215).

The independent conceptual replication provides a useful null/mixed check.
The audit labels it as conceptual rather than strict, identifies randomized
cue selection and task/model changes, and avoids treating a nonsignificant
near-zero mean as proof of equivalence. Its reported aggregate and
benchmark-level directions are tied to Appendix C, Fig. 4. The final TMLR
record identifies the title and authors and its abstract supports the broad
finding of no statistically significant differences for nearly all tested
techniques ([TMLR record](https://openreview.net/forum?id=bgjR5bM44u);
[indexed full-text copy](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=bgjR5bM44u)).

E4 is explicitly classified as indirect: the PNAS study measures how closely
choice distributions resemble human choices in a specific strategic game,
not answer correctness. The primary text confirms that the career-importance
stimulus was one of three zero-shot strategies, with results in SI Figs.
S2–S4, and that the largest models showed little change while some smaller
models shifted without becoming more human-like (Gao et al. 2025, §“Zero-Shot
Prompts”; [primary article](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2501660122),
paras. beginning “Beyond this broader idea” and “Our results are presented”).
This is appropriately weak evidence for the target cue itself.

### Study conditions, provenance, and source quality — meets

The four principal sources are primary empirical reports. The audit records
model families/snapshots, task domains, outcome measures, cue wording, and
important design limits at a level sufficient for the stated focused audit.
It correctly clusters E1 and E2 as one research program, distinguishes the
independent groups behind E3 and E4, and treats arXiv S5 as screened context
rather than direct target evidence. Bibliographic identity and direct links
are provided in the source ledger. The primary records confirm E1's six
model families and task counts (Li et al. 2023, §2.2; primary text lines
113–123) and E2's cue-specific variation (Li et al. 2024, §4.3/Fig. 8(b)).

### Calibration, transfer, and uncertainty — meets

The audit separates measured results from the original authors' proposed
attention/reward explanations (E1), distinguishes subjective human ratings
from correctness, and states the narrow transfer limits of the replication
and PNAS game. It also reports that calibration, time, token, and compute
effects for the target cue were not established. Those are appropriate
uncertainty boundaries for the evidence located.

## Required corrections

None identified.

## Optional recommendations

1. For easier reproduction of the focused search, add the search venues or
   indexes and a compact set of exact query strings to the validation section.
   The audit currently gives search concepts, citation chasing, date, and a
   stop rationale, but not the exact query strings or where they were run.
   This is a documentation improvement, not a blocker for the explicitly
   non-systematic scope.
2. If the root synthesis reuses numerical details from E3, retain the audit's
   caveat that its pooled −0.25 percentage-point result and benchmark-specific
   deltas are estimates from the tested sample/design and do not demonstrate
   equivalence or a universal null effect.

## Review limits

I checked the primary article text and publication records for the material
claims that drive the conclusion. I did not independently rerun the authors'
analyses or conduct a fresh systematic literature search; the assessment is a
review of the audit against SP-01, not a second literature review. The
OpenReview content endpoint was access-challenged during direct opening, so
the final TMLR identification and broad abstract claim were cross-checked via
the indexed publication record; the audit's detailed E3 numeric locations
are recorded as Appendix C, Fig. 4 and remain reproducible from the linked
paper artifact.
