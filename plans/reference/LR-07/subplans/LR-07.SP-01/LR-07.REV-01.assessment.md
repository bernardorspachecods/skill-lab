# Assessment

## Conclusions

**Review result: revise before S2 uses the audit.** The audit otherwise meets
the S1 scope and root completion criteria: it contains 16 official sources,
covers Anthropic and OpenAI/ChatGPT/Codex plus Microsoft and Google, and
includes product documentation, technical guides/cookbooks/examples, and
vendor-authored practice material. It repeatedly separates product capability,
vendor recommendation, vendor-reported outcomes, and independently tested
results. The final Phase 5 re-audit (Re-audit 2) reports C1–C3 passed, closes
the S16 follow-up, and records no unresolved reopen. I found one material C3
omission in S6 that should be reconciled before handoff.

## Evidence

- **Required — complete S6's C3 outcome accounting.** In the S1 audit's
  “Ledger assessments,” S6 is marked for C1–C3; E16 and the C3 findings
  classify its internal launch/adoption status and general benefit language.
  However, the article's opening reports an estimate that the team built the
  product in about one tenth the time it would have taken manually (source
  S6, opening paragraph; reopened at the official article). The audit does
  not identify or classify that explicit vendor-reported estimate. Add it as
  a separate C3 evidence entry, with the article's basis and limits (an
  internal estimate, no comparison design or independent verification), or
  narrow S6's C3 use and explain why the estimate is outside the audit's
  outcome scope. This matters because C3 asks what basis vendor outcome
  statements provide; leaving this reported result unrecorded makes the
  outcome inventory incomplete.
- **Coverage and source roles — pass.** S1–S2 are relevant Anthropic loop and
  verification practice articles; S3 and S16 are runnable OpenAI Cookbook
  examples; S4 is ChatGPT Workspace Agents product documentation; S5 is a
  Codex mechanics explainer; S6–S7 and S15 are vendor internal/practice
  accounts; S8 and S14 are technical guides; S9–S10 are Microsoft framework
  documentation; and S11–S13 provide architecture/workflow guidance from
  Microsoft and Google. Their claimed uses map to KNOW-02 topics, and the
  audit explicitly limits S4 schedules to recurring-run capability rather
  than calling them feedback loops. The Google Discover page (S13) has broad
  marketing-style language, but the audit identifies its source role and
  qualifies its claims rather than using them as measured effects.
- **Evidence fit and boundaries — pass.** I reopened the cited passages for
  the material E entries. Examples include S1's goal stop condition and turn
  cap (E19); S3's deliberately stale example and explicit production stop
  conditions (E15, E6); S4's schedule and default “Always ask” write-action
  setting (E7–E8); S5's tool output being appended and used for the next
  inference (E9); S9's bounded-loop advice and experimental status (E101,
  E107); S11's iteration cap and fallback recommendation (E103, E108); S12's
  human checkpoint and stated rationale (E104, E109, E114); S13's
  self-correction example and broad outcome assertions (E105, E115); S14's
  retry and high-risk-action thresholds (E111); S15's risk-based approval
  policy (E106, E112); and S16's synthetic-data trace/feedback/eval cycle,
  next-cycle continuation, and optional human gates (E17–E18). The audit
  correctly labels examples and advice as vendor documentation, not proof
  they work. It also keeps S7's precision/recall and field-completion
  measures separate and vendor-reported (E12, E20), and identifies S8's
  percentage as hypothetical (E14). No independent test is claimed from
  these first-party sources.
- **Dates, publisher, and version context — pass with one access note.** The
  dated source entries I checked match the official pages; the S4 “updated 12
  days ago” label and S13's ambiguous printed date are transparently
  recorded, and S14 appropriately has no date/version visible in the PDF.
  S9 and S10 currently show an authorization notice in the Microsoft Learn
  rendering while also exposing the cited article text. The content supports
  the audit's claims, but the audit's blanket “full text” access description
  could note that rendering condition so readers can distinguish accessible
  text from the page's access prompt.
- **Search coverage and reconciliation — pass.** Phase 2 names the bounded
  vendor/source-role coverage, excluded broad academic and third-party routes,
  and notes that Anthropic's linked implementation docs and other vendor
  ecosystems were not exhaustively pursued. The stop rationale explains why
  S16 was added, states the 16/20 candidate bound, and avoids an exhaustive
  claim. Re-audit 2 is present and closes the earlier S16 and E17/E18/S9
  corrections; I found no unresolved earlier reopen.

## Uncertainties

- Microsoft Learn's authorization notice may be a limitation of this
  rendering rather than the pages' normal public access behavior. I could
  verify the surfaced article text, but not whether every reader will see it
  without signing in.
- I assessed the bounded audit and its cited official passages, not search
  recall beyond the documented scope. The working log is not part of this
  review target and was not used as a substitute for the audit.

## Recommendations

- Resolve the S6 one-tenth-time estimate before S2 begins. This is the only
  required correction. Preserve the final Re-audit 2 pass record, adding the
  S6 classification as a bounded amendment rather than reopening already
  passed C1/C2 checks unless the change affects them.
- Optional: add a short note to the access/recall limits about the Microsoft
  Learn authorization notice observed during review.
