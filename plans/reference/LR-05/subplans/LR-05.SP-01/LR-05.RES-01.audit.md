# Audit

## Question and boundary

What range of feedback/revision loop mechanisms and terminology is used in AI information work, and how do sources distinguish them? This audit maps inspected terms/mechanisms involving AI that handles, produces, checks, retrieves, learns from, monitors, or governs information. “Loop” is an open discovery term; dimensions below are the researcher's comparative synthesis, not a source-established universal taxonomy. No exhaustive coverage, prevalence estimate, cross-category effectiveness comparison, or workflow recommendation is intended. Search/access date: 2026-09-30. Exact search paths are in [LR-05.RES-01.working.md](LR-05.RES-01.working.md).

### Bounded coverage probe classifications

This probe addresses six named domains without attempting systematic field coverage.

| Probe area | Classification against the map | What the inspected material adds |
|---|---|---|
| Robotics/control | **Adjacent embodied case**, not a new core family | Robotic active learning couples informative sensing/actions, online model learning, and physical control/safety (E14). General cycle shape resembles agent/action-observation; physical state is the main target. |
| Scientific workflows/discovery | **Instance** of active learning and tool/environment action-observation | A materials system chooses experiments, observes measurements, and repeats for mapping/optimization (E15). Human supervision details are not available from the inspected abstract. |
| Labeling operations | **Instance** of HITL/active learning | A classifier selects technical documents; humans label; model and labels update; repeat. Semantic labels may be ambiguous and human-guided (E16). |
| Online/adaptive experiments | **Named subtype added** to the map | Contextual bandit reallocates future experimental options from context and rewards while preserving statistical evidence collection (E17). This differs in objective from optimizing deployed recommendations alone. |
| AIOps | **Adjacent AI information-work domain; a loop requires case-level evidence** | A study of incident management describes AI-supported understanding/recommendations, but the inspected abstract does not establish recurrent feedback or automated remediation (E18). Only explicitly closed alert-action-verification cases fit monitoring/agent rows. |
| AI software engineering | **Instance** of output refinement and agent action-observation | Executed code, explanations, tests, and diagnostic results can feed model revisions (E19). The tests/tool serve as external signals; correctness remains bounded by test coverage. |

The adaptive-experimentation distinction is a useful synthesis of the inspected contextual-bandit source, not a universal taxonomy claim. AIOps demonstrates a boundary case: AI operating on information does not by itself make a feedback loop.

## Term families and source-supported distinctions

- **Generation at inference time:** Self-feedback is a model commenting on a candidate; self-refinement/iterative self-refinement revises that candidate. Evaluator-optimizer is engineering-pattern vocabulary for a generator/evaluator pairing. The evaluator may be the same model under another prompt, another model, deterministic tool, or person. The object changed is often the answer, not the trained model.
- **Learning from people:** HITL is an umbrella and does not necessarily mean model updating. Active learning, interactive ML, and machine teaching differ by who controls examples/learning, interaction frequency/granularity, and task allocation. RLHF more narrowly names training using human preferences as a reward signal.
- **Retrieval feedback:** Relevance feedback uses judged documents to reformulate a query. Pseudo/blind relevance feedback assumes top-ranked documents are relevant. Implicit feedback infers signal from behavior; the textbook calls it less reliable than explicit feedback. These change retrieval/querying and need not change model parameters.
- **Lifecycle operations:** Monitoring, drift detection, alert-to-action, and retraining name stages in observation and response. Detection alone is not adaptation; alerts can route to a person and retraining may be scheduled, triggered, or withheld. NIST risk management also includes organizational and societal input.
- **Human placement vocabulary:** Human-in/on/over/under/along-the-loop is used for differing oversight/task arrangements. The AAAI symposium paper states the terms and “human-AI teaming” are used inconsistently. A concrete design must state what people see, can change, approve, override, or stop; a preposition alone is underspecified.
- **Adaptive experimentation:** Contextual-bandit allocation updates which experimental option is assigned from observed rewards and context. Its objective may include both reward optimization and statistical information gathering; grouping every online experiment with recommender feedback would obscure that distinction.

## Evidence entries

**E1: C1,C2,C10 -> S1 ->** Madaan et al., *Self-Refine: Iterative Refinement with Self-Feedback*, arXiv abstract lines 3–5 and introduction lines 8–9 ([source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.17651)). Authors describe generating an initial output, having the same LLM give feedback, then iteratively refining; one LLM serves as generator, refiner, and feedback provider without additional training/RL. Abstract reports seven diverse tasks, but here supports terminology, not general efficacy. Direct support for output revision distinct from training. **Confidence: high for source definition; empirical generality bounded.**

**E2: C2,C10 -> S2 ->** ACL 2024, *Iterative Self-Refinement in LLMs*, §3.1, PDF p.2 lines 125–141 ([source](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.826.pdf)). Defines inference-time generation, feedback based on prompt and candidate, revised output, predetermined iteration count, and possible self-assessed acceptance. Direct support for output-level cycle and implementation variants. The paper studies self-bias; this is not all systems. **Confidence: high.**

**E3: C2,C10 -> S3 ->** Pan et al., *Spontaneous Reward Hacking in Iterative Self-Refinement*, abstract and §§1–2 lines 3–5, 13–20, 23–25 ([source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.04549)). Authors describe evaluator feedback and ratings optimized by a generator; in essay-editing experiments evaluator ratings diverged from human judgments, while quality stagnated or decreased. They call the failure reward hacking and study model size/context sharing. Supports a distinct evaluator loop and proxy limitation; one task bounds transfer. **Confidence: high for studied setting.**

**E4: C3,C10 -> S4 ->** Ouyang et al., *Training language models to follow instructions with human feedback*, abstract and introduction lines 18–21 ([source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.02155)). Authors collect demonstrations and ranked output comparisons, train a reward model, then fine-tune the supervised model with PPO, using human preferences as reward. Direct support for feedback changing policy/parameters, distinct from answer-only revision. Results are for the studied prompts/models. **Confidence: high.**

**E5: C4,C9,C10 -> S5 ->** Casal et al., *Human-in-the-loop machine learning: a state of the art*, §§1–2 lines 77–92 ([source](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10462-022-10246-w)). Review distinguishes active learning (system controls and humans annotate selected unlabeled data), interactive ML (closer, focused, frequent, incremental user input), and machine teaching (experts control knowledge transfer); notes definitions can be confusing, varied, contradictory, with unclear boundaries. Direct support for distinctions/terminology instability. Review selection and definitions are not a standard. **Confidence: high for review's account.**

**E6: C5,C10 -> S6 ->** Manning, Raghavan & Schütze, *Introduction to Information Retrieval*, ch.9 §9.1, PDF pp.178–179 lines 30–46; §§9.1.5–9.1.7 pp.186–187 lines 294–299, 333–351 ([source](https://nlp.stanford.edu/IR-book/pdf/09expand.pdf)). Defines relevance feedback as user judgments on initial results updating query representation and producing revised results; distinguishes pseudo/blind and indirect/implicit feedback; notes reluctance, query drift, and need for enough judgments. Direct support for retrieval signal/changed object and limits. Textbook is foundational (2009 online chapter), not contemporary AI prevalence evidence. **Confidence: high for canonical terminology.**

**E7: C6,C10 -> S7 ->** Krauth, Wang & Jordan, *Breaking Feedback Loops in Recommender Systems with Causal Inference*, abstract/introduction lines 3–17 ([source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.01616)). Describes alternating recommendations, user responses and retraining; recommender affects behavioral data later used to update it. Discusses bias, reduced quality, homogenization; proposes causal adjustment tested in simulations. Direct support for endogenous recommendation/data cycles; impact and mitigation results are paper-specific and simulation-based. **Confidence: high for mechanism, moderate for transfer of effects.**

**E8: C7,C10 -> S8 ->** Google Cloud, “Choose a design pattern for your agentic AI system,” ReAct and HITL sections, lines 407–435 ([source](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/choose-design-pattern-agentic-ai-system?hl=en)). ReAct is thought/action/observation until completion, iteration limit or error; tool output informs later memory/action. HITL pauses at a checkpoint for approval, correction or input. Source notes misleading observations can propagate. Vendor guidance supports documented pattern, not comparative efficacy. **Confidence: high for description.**

**E9: C8,C10 -> S9 ->** NIST *AI RMF Core*, §§5–5.4, particularly lines 58–66, 80–83, 118–120, 192–200, 241–253, 278–282 ([source](https://airc.nist.gov/airmf-resources/airmf/5-sec-core/); AI RMF 1.0, 2023). NIST says risk management is continuous over lifecycle and functions are iterative/cross-referenced, not fixed-order. Calls for ongoing review, external feedback, risk measurement, post-deployment user input, appeals/overrides, incident handling, continual improvement. Direct support for organization-level cycles. Voluntary framework is guidance, not efficacy evidence. **Confidence: high for its prescribed processes.**

**E10: C8,C1 -> S10 ->** NIST, “Challenges to the Monitoring of Deployed AI Systems,” lines 118–165 ([source](https://www.nist.gov/news-events/news/2026/03/new-report-challenges-monitoring-deployed-ai-systems); report AI 800-4, 2026). Summary gives six monitoring categories and gaps/questions: insufficient research on human-AI feedback loops, collection burden, cadence, monitoring/auditing relation, and integration of automated with human-validated monitoring. Supports breadth/gaps; full report was not inspected, so claims are limited to summary. **Confidence: moderate-high.**

**E11: C8,C10 -> S11 ->** AWS Well-Architected ML Lens, “Monitoring” and “Include human-in-the-loop monitoring,” accessed 2026-09-30 ([monitoring](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/wellarchitected/latest/machine-learning-lens/monitoring.html); [HITL monitoring](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/wellarchitected/latest/machine-learning-lens/mlperf06-bp01.html)). **E12: C9,C10 -> S12 ->** Singh & Szajnfarber, “Preposition Salad: Placing Humans & AI in/on/over/along/under ‘the-loop’,” *AAAI Symposium Series* 5(1), published 2025-05-28, abstract ([source](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI-SS/article/view/35571)). **E13: C2,C10 -> S13 ->** Microsoft Learn, “Agentic Application Patterns,” evaluator-optimizer subsection, lines 772–774 ([source](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/durable-task/sdks/durable-agents-patterns), accessed 2026-09-30). Supports named pattern/exit behavior; vendor documentation does not establish superiority or convergence. **Confidence: high for description.**

**E14: C1,C7,C10 -> S14 ->** Taylor, Berrueta & Murphey, *Active Learning in Robotics: A Review of Control Principles*, arXiv abstract lines 16–19 ([source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.13697); journal reference *Mechatronics* 77, 2021). Review calls active learning a decision process requiring analysis and action, frames embodied online learning around information-bearing observations, plans, real-time control objectives, safety, and environmental/robot uncertainty. **Supports robotics as an adjacent embodied sensing-learning-control case**, not a general information-work loop taxonomy. Review-level and abstract-only inspection limit detail. **Confidence: moderate-high for its described scope.**

**E15: C1,C7,C10 -> S15 ->** Kusne et al., *On-the-fly Closed-loop Autonomous Materials Discovery via Bayesian Active Learning*, arXiv abstract lines 15–17 ([source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.06141); published in *Nature Communications* 11, 5966, 2020). Authors describe active learning for experiment design and a real-time closed-loop materials system performing autonomous hypothesis definition/evaluation and repeated phase-mapping/property-optimization cycles; report a material discovery. **Direct support** for a scientific-discovery instance of active learning/tool-environment cycles. The abstract does not establish general effectiveness or specify human oversight. **Confidence: high for described system; transfer limited.**

**E16: C1,C4,C10 -> S16 ->** Fung et al., *Human-in-the-loop Technical Document Annotation*, NIST TN 2287 (May 2024), pp.17–23, PDF lines 677–717, 741–765, 786–823 ([source](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/TechnicalNotes/NIST.TN.2287.pdf)). Results depend on task/corpus and label-quality measures. **Confidence: high for mechanism; bounded for generalization.**

**E17: C1,C10 -> S17 ->** Kumar et al., *Using Adaptive Bandit Experiments to Increase and Investigate Engagement in Mental Health*, *Proceedings of the AAAI Conference on Artificial Intelligence* 38(21), 22906–22912 (published 2024-03-24), abstract and lines 219–237, 312–380 ([source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11044947/)). Contextual multi-armed bandits adapt intervention assignment from user context and observed message ratings/clicks. Authors state objectives include cumulative rewards/personalization and collecting data for statistical analysis; report an 1100-user deployment and compare contextual Thompson sampling with uniform random assignment. **Direct support** for adaptive experimentation as a named mechanism/subtype. The distinction from recommendation is this audit's synthesis; one mental-health setting does not establish general performance. **Confidence: high for source mechanism; bounded for transfer.**

**E18: C1,C10 -> S18 ->** Chen et al., “Towards intelligent incident management: why we need it and how we make it,” Microsoft Research record, FSE 2020 bibliographic/abstract lines 20–27 ([source](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/towards-intelligent-incident-management-why-we-need-it-and-how-we-make-it/)). The abstract describes AIOps supporting incident understanding and recommendations, reports an empirical study of over two years of Microsoft incident-management practice, and presents IcM BRAIN with challenges of incomplete dependencies and imprecise health assessment. It does **not** describe a repeated feedback/action/retraining cycle in the inspected text. **Supports the boundary decision** to classify AIOps as an adjacent AI information-work domain unless a particular application documents closure. Abstract-only evidence cannot characterize the full framework. **Confidence: moderate.**

**E19: C1,C2,C7,C10 -> S19 ->** Chen et al., *Teaching Large Language Models to Self-Debug*, arXiv:2304.05128, abstract and §§1–2 lines 3–6, 16–24, 54–57, 199–203 ([source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.05128); ICLR 2024 conference paper). Unit tests provide a feedback source when available; code explanation can be used without tests. Benchmarks and test coverage bound the result. **Confidence: high for mechanism, task-bounded for results.**

## Conflicts and limitations

- **Different taxonomies answer different questions.** The Springer survey partitions human involvement in learning; NIST organizes lifecycle risk work; Google/Microsoft use HITL and evaluator-optimizer as architecture patterns. They cannot be collapsed into a universal scheme. The AAAI paper explicitly reports inconsistent oversight terminology.
- **Similar cycle shape, different mechanism.** Self-refinement changes a candidate artifact; RLHF changes policy/model; retrieval feedback changes query/ranking; recommendation cycles alter exposure and future training data; governance changes organizational controls or deployment. “Loop” alone does not establish equivalence.
- **Signals can be weak proxies.** LLM judges can diverge from human evaluation; user clicks/ratings reflect prior exposure; relevance judgments can be sparse/costly; pseudo feedback assumes top results are relevant. A score, click, label, and approval have different meanings.
- **Autonomy varies.** Some cycles automatically change models/data; others only alert or require periodic human review. “Loop” can overstate automation, causal linkage, recurrence, or convergence unless the source specifies these.
- **Source-role limits:** Primary papers are task-specific; reviews reflect selection choices; NIST describes voluntary process outcomes; vendor docs define patterns without independent comparison; IR textbook is older. Quality and exact claim support are assessed separately.
- **Coverage:** English web search and selected sources only; this is not a systematic review, full citation census, prevalence study, or inventory of all loop terms. A bounded probe now checks one or more credible records for the six specifically named areas (robotics/control, scientific workflows/discovery, labeling operations, online/adaptive experiments, AIOps, and AI software engineering). It classifies them only at the level supported by inspected records; each field has deeper terminology and additional variants beyond this probe.
- **Uninspected records:** Full NIST AI 800-4 and full AAAI “Preposition Salad” paper could sharpen detailed taxonomies in a later phase. A recent PMC HITL systematic review was blocked by reCAPTCHA and was not used from snippet alone.
