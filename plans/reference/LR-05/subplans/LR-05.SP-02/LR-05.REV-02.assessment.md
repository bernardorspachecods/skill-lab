# LR-05.SP-02 output review

## Decision

**No required corrections.** The audit meets the SP-02 scope and completion criteria. Its application map covers the reviewed S1 families through supported cases, calibrates outcome claims to each study's design, and clearly states that the evidence reviewed does not establish that monitoring followed by remediation improves outcomes.

## Findings by criterion

### Application coverage

**Meets.** The audit covers output refinement, action/execution feedback, preference learning, retrieval, human annotation, adaptive experimentation, recommender feedback, monitoring/governance, and scientific experiment selection (C1–C9; findings at lines 35–43). This is a reasonable representative map rather than an exhaustive inventory, consistent with the stated scope at lines 11–17 and stop rationale at line 105. The scientific case is a useful adjacent extension rather than a claim that S1's map is exhaustive.

### Case-level support and evidence distinctions

**Meets.** The cases identify mechanisms, feedback sources, outcomes, and setting-specific limits, with linked E# entries and source locations. Benchmark findings are explicitly bounded to the evaluated tasks and datasets (E1, E3, E5); the adaptive-intervention results distinguish simulated reward/power/FPR estimates from the field response-rate observation (C6, E7); recommendation correction is identified as simulation evidence (C7, E8); clinical drift is observational evidence (E10); and Stanford/NIST material is treated as documented workflow or guidance (E9, E11). The abstract-only inspection of Pan et al. and CAMEO is disclosed (E2, E12; line 98).

The audit generally keeps source-reported results separate from broader interpretation. For example, it does not present benchmark gains as production reliability, a reported materials discovery as a comparative loop effect, or monitoring plans as evidence of improved outcomes.

### Monitoring-to-remediation outcome gap

**Meets; key gap is handled accurately.** C8 and its evidence entries distinguish plans and recommendations from measured effects (lines 30, 42, 63–67). The audit expressly says the PLOS observational study measured fragility and monitoring signals, not remediation effects (E10), and that none of the reviewed monitoring sources measures whether a complete monitoring/remediation/governance cycle causally improves clinical outcomes (line 94). The source roles support that boundary: Stanford is implementation documentation from the implementing institution; PLOS is a retrospective observational study; NIST sources are a framework and a workshop/literature synthesis. No reviewed source is represented as an intervention evaluation of monitoring-triggered remediation.

### Negative, null, and mixed findings

**Meets.** The audit retains self-refinement failures and proxy-reward harms (E1–E2), unstable single-reward RAG results (E5), annotation quality cautions (E6), sparse ratings and simulated tradeoffs plus a field null result for one intervention arm (E7), and post-deployment clinical drift (E10). These are summarized in the refutation and conflict sections (lines 93–97, 104), with appropriate limits on generalization.

### Source quality, provenance, independence, and uncertainty

**Meets.** Source quality, claim fit, and independence/overlap are treated separately in the ledger (lines 73–89). The audit identifies developer-evaluator overlap for InstructGPT, NIST institutional overlap, the Stanford implementation source's preprint status and self-report role, and the lack of independent replications where relevant. It does not treat multiple URLs or artifacts as independent corroboration. Limits from abstract-only access, anonymized systems, and non-systematic selection are visible (lines 98–99).

### Conflicts, limitations, and stopping rationale

**Meets.** Conflicting positive and adverse refinement findings are reconciled by task, feedback, evaluator, and endpoint rather than averaged into one general effect (line 93). The audit separates benchmark, simulation, observational, documentary, and normative evidence (lines 94–97), names material coverage gaps (line 105), and gives a plausible representative-coverage stopping rationale without claiming exhaustive recall.

## Required corrections

None identified.

## Optional advice

No change is needed for SP-02 acceptance. In later synthesis, preserve the current narrow interpretation of the clinical monitoring evidence: it supports post-deployment fragility and the observability of operational signals, while the causal effect of monitoring-triggered remediation remains unestablished in this source set.

## Uncertainties

This review assessed the audit against the SP-02 brief and its cited source descriptions, with focused source checking of the monitoring evidence. It is not an independent re-extraction of all underlying papers or a systematic search for every application. Accordingly, it confirms the audit's source-bound framing and coverage, not the completeness of the open literature or every numerical result in every case.
