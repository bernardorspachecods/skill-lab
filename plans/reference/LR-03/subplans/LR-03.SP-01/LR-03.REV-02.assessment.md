## Conclusions

**Pass.** The revised `Workflow → 1. Define the brief` guidance satisfies the
SP-01 completion criteria and is consistent with the agreed Outcome. It
separates the user's task from its topic, captures context conditionally,
distinguishes stated facts from assumptions, and limits clarification to
ambiguities that could materially change research direction or conclusion. It
does not add a speculative questionnaire or duplicate the later scope and
output instructions.

The RES-02 audit's material source descriptions and evidence-to-claim links
are accurate within the stated transfer limits. Its central policy is
appropriately labeled as a design recommendation/inference, not as a result
directly established by the literature. No required correction was found.

## Evidence

- **Subplan criteria and Outcome:** Compared the changed section in
  `agent-skills/skills/research/SKILL.md` with each SP-01 completion criterion
  and its Outcome. The edit names the user's actual question/task and relevant
  decision or learning need; makes consumer and use context conditional on
  whether they affect relevance or interpretation; marks assumptions; keeps
  stated scope, geography, time period, stakes, and constraints when they
  matter; and asks only when resolving ambiguity could materially change the
  research direction or conclusion. Existing claim decomposition and support
  tracking remain in the same brief step. This meets the criterion to fit the
  current brief without taking on query breadth, source selection, or output
  design.
- **Root completion criteria:** This focused edit preserves the skill's
  existing evidence matrix and brief structure. SP-01 alone cannot establish
  that the eventual five-stage workflow is integrated; the root plan leaves
  that for later subplans and the final integration check. No conflict with
  the root criteria was found in this change.
- **S1 / Zamani et al. (E1):** Checked the full WWW 2020 paper and its online
  experiment section. It describes short, ambiguous search queries and
  clarification as a way to resolve information needs. The experiment used
  two EN-US treatments of 2.5 million users each for one week and reports
  48.57% relative CTR engagement uplift (paired t-test, p < 10^-20) for a
  clarification question compared with a static title plus the same candidate
  answers. The audit accurately describes the outcome as engagement, not
  research-answer quality, and limits transfer to LLM research briefing.
- **S2 / Maguitman (E2):** Checked the journal record and review abstract.
  They support task context influencing query generation/refinement or
  disambiguation, result filtering/ranking, and search-engine choice. The audit
  correctly treats intended consumer, constraints, and use as design prompts,
  not universally necessary fields validated by this review.
- **S3 / Zhang & Choi (E3):** Checked the ACL Anthology record and paper PDF.
  The paper evaluates deciding when to clarify in QA, machine translation,
  and natural-language inference; its framework considers interaction cost
  and whether one interpretation dominates. The audit correctly says this
  supports selective clarification factors, while its operational
  “materially change direction or conclusion” threshold and application to
  open-web research remain an inference.
- **S4 / Yang et al. (E4):** Checked the current arXiv v3 full text. It reports
  8.4k model/prompt/requirement observations across three tasks, variable
  behavior on unspecified requirements, and degraded performance when many
  requirements are specified together in its experiments. The audit correctly
  limits transfer: this is prompt underspecification work using curated
  prompts and tasks, not a human-led web-research clarification study. Its
  findings support caution both against assumed inference and against an
  exhaustive intake checklist.
- **S5 / Huang et al. (E5):** Checked the arXiv full text. The preprint reports
  5,251 problems and 9,006 task instances in five domains; it says tested
  models underestimate missing information, fail to find minimal sufficient
  query sets, and often stop before acquiring enough information. It
  explicitly studies underspecification rather than ambiguity and describes
  controlled evaluation tasks. The audit preserves those distinctions and
  does not present the results as rates for open-web research.
- The audit's evidence-backed synthesis maps E1–E5 to their respective claims
  and labels the material-ambiguity rule as an inference. Its stated gap—that
  no located source directly tests the proposed threshold in an LLM web
  research workflow—is consistent with the scope of the reviewed sources.

## Uncertainties

No material uncertainty affects the pass finding. The exact operational
threshold for “materially change” is a judgment call, and the audit already
states that no reviewed study empirically calibrates it for open-web research.
The sources also do not validate each proposed context field as necessary in
every task.
