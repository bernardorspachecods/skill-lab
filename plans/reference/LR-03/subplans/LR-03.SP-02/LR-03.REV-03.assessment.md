After the executor completes and the coordinator reconciles this subplan,
independently assess the scope and stopping guidance in

## Conclusions

**Pass; no required corrections.** The SP-02 additions turn the SP-01 oriented
goal into a workable bounded scope. They prioritize by likely effect on the
user's outcome and dependencies, make temporal/geographic/population limits
conditional on whether they can change the answer, and require a reason for
material exclusions. The stop rule checks adequacy for question importance,
alternatives, uncertainty, and the likely material value of another search
path. It explicitly rejects open-web completeness claims. The additions fit
the brief and do not duplicate source-selection or consumer-output guidance.

The supporting audit's material source descriptions and evidence-to-claim
links are accurate within its stated limits. Its empirical and methodological
evidence is kept distinct from proposed workflow heuristics, and the audit
does not claim that a universal prioritization/stopping rule or exhaustive
open-web recall has been validated.

## Evidence

- **SP-02 completion criteria and brief:** The brief asks for bounded,
  prioritized questions, answer-relevant constraints, proportionate evidence
  needs, and stopping conditions, while leaving source hierarchy and answer
  structure to other stages. In `SKILL.md`, `Workflow → 1. Define the brief`
  ranks questions by outcome impact and dependencies, limits secondary
  context and constraints to matters that could change interpretation or the
  answer, asks for reasons for material exclusions, and scales evidence
  expectations to importance/stakes. `Workflow → 2. Plan and discover`
  operationalizes adequate support; `Workflow → 4. Synthesize and audit`
  weighs unresolved uncertainty and whether another path could materially
  change the answer. Both sections say this is task-sensitive and the skill
  explicitly says not to imply complete open-web recall. This makes the
  priority and stop checks actionable without setting a universal source
  count or recall threshold.
- **SP-01 dependency and separation:** The completed SP-01 Outcome orients
  research to the user's actual task/decision or learning need and identifies
  context only where it affects relevance or interpretation. SP-02 explicitly
  builds on this oriented goal. Its separate placement before discovery is
  coherent; it neither revises source evaluation nor consumer-facing output.
  Source mapping remains in `Plan and discover`, claim extraction in
  `Evaluate and extract`, and answer shape in `Output`.
- **E1 / S1:** The ACL Anthology proceedings PDF identifies the NAACL-HLT
  2018 paper and reports precision@1 increasing from 20.8 to 27.5 on
  COMPLEXWEBQUESTIONS (abstract and evaluation, PDF pp. 641–642). The dataset
  was generated from compositional questions and paraphrases; the paper itself
  notes an artificial question distribution. The audit accurately confines
  support to decomposition in this benchmark and notes it does not test
  user-directed broad research or prioritization.
- **E2 / S2:** Cochrane Handbook Chapter 2 says upfront scoping can set
  sensible boundaries and avoid unmanageable quantities (section 2.2),
  discusses scope tradeoffs, and ties breadth to relevance, impact,
  applicability, user needs, context, and resources (sections 2.3.1–2.4.2).
  This supports purposeful scoping within systematic reviews; the audit
  appropriately avoids presenting it as a universal, experimentally tested
  web-research rule.
- **E3 / S3:** PRISMA 2020 explanation, item 5, asks authors to specify
  eligibility characteristics (including study design/setting and report
  characteristics such as publication year/language) and recommends
  rationales for notable restrictions. The audit accurately treats this as
  systematic-review reporting guidance that indirectly informs explicit,
  justified scope constraints.
- **E4 / S4:** Cochrane Handbook Chapter 4, section 4.4.11, says objective
  stopping is difficult, candidate rules have had little formal evaluation,
  scarce evidence warrants caution, known-item checking alone is insufficient,
  and citation/reference checks help assess performance. It also describes
  qualitative-search saturation and complex topics where exhaustive retrieval
  may not be the priority. The audit's account and transfer limits match the
  source.
- **E5 / S5:** The ACL Anthology record for the 2019 EMNLP-IJCNLP paper gives
  the authors, venue, date, pages, and abstract. The abstract describes a
  Poisson-process stopping method allowing desired recall and probability
  levels, evaluated on a public dataset. The audit correctly limits this to
  collection-based retrieval and reports no detailed performance figures;
  its warning that this does not establish open-web recall is sound.
- **E6 / S3:** PRISMA items 6–7 say reporting sources and last-searched dates
  helps readers assess review completeness/currency and that full search
  strategies improve transparency, replication, and assessment of likely
  identification. The audit correctly uses this for reporting provenance,
  not as direct evidence of a stopping rule.
- **Source verification performed:** Opened the ACL PDF for S1 and the
  publisher/authoritative pages for S2–S5. Checked the relevant passages in
  Cochrane Chapters 2 and 4, PRISMA items 5–7, the ACL S5 abstract/metadata,
  and S1's reported result and dataset description. These checks confirmed
  the key descriptions and linked support cited above.

## Uncertainties

No material source-to-claim error found. The central transfer limitation
remains material but is already stated: none of the cited studies or methods
directly validates this complete prioritization and stopping workflow for
LLM-led, user-directed open-web research. “Prioritize by how much answers
could change the outcome” is a reasonable operational heuristic, not a
tested scoring formula; the skill does not present it as one.

## Recommendations

No required corrections. Keep the current distinctions between supported
methodological advice and workflow judgment, and retain the explicit caveat
that search stopping does not establish complete open-web recall.
