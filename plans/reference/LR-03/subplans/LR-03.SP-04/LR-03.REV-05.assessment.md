## Evidence

- **Question → claim → evidence → conclusion:** The skill directs the
  researcher to derive material claims from prioritized questions, extract
  passages/data with a location, link each item as supporting, partly
  supporting, conflicting, or unresolved, then answer each question from the
  linked evidence. Missing evidence remains a gap; source summaries cannot
  stand in for claim support. The E# record specifies claim and source IDs,
  exact evidence and location, relation, limits, support status, and
  confidence. These steps meet SP-04's extraction, direct-finding, and exit
  criteria.
- **Limits and synthesis distinctions:** The skill asks what each item does
  and does not establish in light of wording, method, population, period, and
  other scope limits. It retains contrary and absent support, groups duplicate
  provenance, distinguishes source-reported results from cross-source
  inference, and separates analysis and recommendations with their added
  assumptions. The traceability limitation explicitly avoids promising
  accuracy or overclaim prevention. Its guidance fits SP-02's prioritized,
  task-bounded scope and SP-03's paths to accountable/original records,
  citations, and disconfirming evidence.
- **No premature output layout:** The SP-04 diff is confined to extraction,
  synthesis, and stopping guidance. The pre-existing `## Output` section is
  unchanged; no final consumer-facing structure is added in this unit.
- **E1 (CLAIM-BENCH): verified.** The official paper reports 346 manually
  annotated claim–evidence pairs, over-four-times retrieved pairs versus the
  single-pass baseline with improved recall and significant computational
  cost; its methods describe the staged and one-by-one approaches. The prompt
  appendix requests evidence locations, strength, limitations, and exact
  quotes. The audit appropriately limits transfer and notes the
  precision–recall trade-off. [ACL Anthology record and paper](https://aclanthology.org/2025.ijcnlp-long.127/)
- **E2 (EXPERTQA): substantive claims and corrected page range verified.** The
  paper evaluates questions from 484 participants across 32 fields and
  separates question informativeness, cite-worthiness, factuality, evidence
  faithfulness, and source reliability. It reports missing attributions for
  18% of cite-worthy claims in retrieve-and-read systems, more incomplete
  attributions for post-hoc systems, and plausible-domain citations whose
  page contents mismatched more than 60% of the time in the stated GPT-4/no-
  retrieval setting. Its limitations include non-atomic sentence claims,
  incomplete field coverage, and the sampled English-speaking regions. These
  results support exact attribution checks, not a causal effect of labels on
  clarity. The corrected audit citation now matches the official record's
  pp. **3025–3045**. [ACL Anthology record and paper](https://aclanthology.org/2024.naacl-long.167/)
- **E3 (LAB): verified.** The official paper reports that large/fine-tuned
  models did best by citing input documents, small prompted models could
  benefit from post-hoc retrieval, and evidence quality predicted response
  quality for single-fact but not multi-fact responses. The audit accurately
  confines this to long-document tasks and does not claim a universal
  extraction workflow. [ACL Anthology record and paper](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.463/)
- **E4 (NHMRC): verified.** The live guidance says good synthesis does not
  merely list findings; it should use clear systematic methods, assess
  certainty, and convey valid overall conclusions. It ties synthesis
  priorities and evidence types to the questions. Its systematic-review and
  guideline scope is acknowledged in the audit. [NHMRC guidance](https://www.nhmrc.gov.au/guidelinesforguidelines/develop/synthesising-evidence)
- **E5 (JBI): verified.** The current manual page is marked updated 5 May
  2025; it says conclusions answer review questions from results/direct
  inference, while practice recommendations also depend on generalizability
  and applicability. The audit correctly treats this as methods guidance,
  not a study of reader effects, and acknowledges its systematic-review
  transfer limit. [JBI Manual](https://jbi-global.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/MANUAL/pages/355828997/4.4.10%2BConclusions%2Band%2Brecommendations)

Verification used the cited publisher records and full-text PDFs for E1–E3,
and the NHMRC and JBI guidance pages for E4–E5. The root and SP-04
completion criteria, SP-02 and SP-03 Outcomes, SP-04 brief and Outcome, audit,
and focused skill diff were checked. No search snippets were treated as
evidence.

## Uncertainties

The review confirmed the audit's own transfer limits: the empirical sources
cover bounded scientific-paper, expert QA, and long-document settings, while
NHMRC/JBI address formal evidence synthesis. They do not validate this whole
workflow for arbitrary web research, nor show that explicit limit fields
causally prevent overclaiming or improve reader calibration. This uncertainty
is accurately represented in the audit and skill. The former E2 page-range
mismatch has been corrected in the audit and independently verified against
the official ACL Anthology record.
