## Evidence

- **Integrated workflow and earlier outcomes:** SP-01 orients work around the
  actual task and material ambiguity; SP-02 prioritizes bounded questions and
  sets task-sensitive evidence and stopping expectations; SP-03 maps useful
  sources and search paths; SP-04 links exact source evidence to claims and
  synthesizes direct findings with gaps and confidence. The current skill's
  Workflow sections 1–4 carry these decisions forward in that order. Output
  refers back to the same claim/evidence IDs and preserves the separation of
  source-reported results, inference, and recommendations.
- **Answer structure and support:** Output directs the researcher to lead with
  findings, select groupings by user need, keep use-changing qualifications
  near findings, and place citations or an unambiguous evidence-record link
  near claims. It distinguishes detailed evidence records from disposable
  search notes and limits full claim matrices/source ledgers to standard and
  high-stakes work when traceability, review, or assignment requires them.
  Lightweight work gets concise sources, dates, uncertainty, and caveats.
  Confidence is expressed in relation to evidence fit and limits; a label or
  score is optional and must be meaningful. These instructions are consistent
  with SP-05 and root criteria and do not make one answer template mandatory.
- **Example:** The “best practices grouped by topic or situation” example is
  explicitly labeled illustrative, uses bracketed placeholders, and says to
  leave them unfilled rather than invent findings or evidence. It demonstrates
  the requested direct finding, local caveat/source ID, and optional separate
  evidence record without asserting a factual practice.
- **Audit evidence checked independently:** EFSA's guidance says uncertainty
  information should be placed for transparency/accessibility and recommends
  a concise uncertainty summary alongside detailed methods/results
  ([source](https://multimedia.efsa.europa.eu/uncertainty-tutorial/uncertainty-analysis/reporting.html),
  lines 32–44), supporting E1's bounded summary-plus-detail claim. The FAccT
  2025 paper reports its final 21-participant expert study, concerns about
  insufficient detail (21/21), overconfident language (16/21), missing claim
  citations (18/21), misattribution/misinterpretation (21/21), and sources
  listed but not used (13/21); the audit correctly presents these as
  qualitative, answer-engine-specific observations, not universal consumer
  preferences ([paper](https://facctconference.org/static/docs/facct2025-206archivalpdfs/facct2025-final493-acmpaginated.pdf),
  pp. 3–5, Table 1).
- The AAAI 2025 experiment matches E3's 303 Prolific participants, 0/1/5
  citation conditions, valid/random citations, ten questions, higher trust with
  citations, no significant trust difference between one and five citations,
  and 193 of 1,976 cited answers manually checked. It also finds random
  citations less trusted than valid citations, consistent with the audit's
  explicit nuance and non-harmlessness caveat ([paper](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/34550/36705),
  pp. 1–4, 6).
- Li and Aral's arXiv study reports a preregistered randomized experiment,
  total sample of 4,927, nine search topics, increased trust/sharing from
  reference links regardless of link validity, lower trust/sharing under
  uncertainty highlighting, and an association between trust, more clicks,
  and less evaluation time. The audit distinguishes these reliance outcomes
  from correctness and notes its preprint/task limits ([paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.06435),
  abstract and results §§3–4).
- Steyvers et al. report that longer explanations increased confidence
  without improving discrimination, and that aligning uncertainty wording to
  model confidence reduced calibration/discrimination gaps. The audit states
  this as a bounded QA-task finding and explicitly cautions that model
  confidence is not evidence quality ([article](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42256-024-00976-7),
  abstract and Results).
- DeepResearch Bench's final ICLR 2026 paper supports E6's task-adaptive RACE
  criteria, separate report-quality versus citation-quality measures, 100
  tasks across 22 fields, and human consistency study over 50 Chinese tasks,
  four agents, and 70+ master's-level expert annotators (three per task).
  The final text supports the readability/direct-response descriptions and
  explicitly describes FACT as a complementary observational metric because
  citation parsing failed for some systems and crawler access or fetched
  content affected measurements; overall rankings use RACE. These details
  match revised E6 ([final ICLR/OpenReview paper](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=hQ0K2Hhq7H),
  §§3.1–3.2, 4.2–4.3, Appendix C). ICLR's 2026 program lists the work and
  final author list, matching the revised ledger ([ICLR listing](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2026/day/4/23)).
  The earlier arXiv v1 has a different author list and preprint status, which
  the revised ledger correctly records as historical context ([arXiv version
  history](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.11763)). The audit's stated limits—that
  this is a benchmark rather than a consumer layout study and that its human
  consistency evidence is restricted to Chinese tasks and expert annotators—
  remain appropriately bounded.
- **Frontmatter and references:** The skill package frontmatter is valid under
  `validate_skill_catalog.py`; that validator marks the `research` package
  `valid` despite unrelated catalog-wide issues. The relative
  `references/academic-research-mode.md` target exists.
