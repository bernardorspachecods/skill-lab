# Assessment

## Conclusions

**Initial review outcome: correction required. Final review outcome: passes SP-04.** The RESULT is a coherent, readable standalone mini-book with a useful navigation map, a logical progression from concepts to cases and guidance, clear evidence boundaries, and extensive links to the reviewed S1–S3 audits. It generally separates demonstrated effects, observed practices, cross-case inference, and recommendation, and handles mixed, adverse, null-like, and unlocated evidence carefully. The initial stopping/control gap has been resolved as recorded in Re-review below.

## Evidence

- **Case stopping and control — required correction.** SP-04 requires each included case to explain stopping and user role “where evidence permits” (PLAN.md, Completion criteria, lines 21–36 and 58–63). The checkpoint case reports completion-time differences and says the tasks were bounded, but does not state that the task ended at completion (RESULT, lines 68–72); S2 E2 explicitly says “task ends at completion” and describes the user confirming, correcting, and triggering redo ([S2 audit, E2, line 27](../../../../knowledge/audit/KNOW-02.AUD-02.md#L27)). The CowPilot case describes pause/takeover controls and says there was no general stopping policy, but omits its observed stopping point (RESULT, lines 76–80); S2 E4 states that stopping was task completion or continued user takeover and that no universal rule was tested ([S2 audit, E4, line 31](../../../../knowledge/audit/KNOW-02.AUD-02.md#L31)). Please add these task-level stop/control facts while retaining the caveat that neither study establishes a general stopping rule. In the research/analysis cases, where S1–S2 do not report a user-specific stopping criterion, identify it as unreported rather than leaving the stopping dimension implicit (RESULT, lines 90–98).
- **Standalone usefulness and navigation — supported.** The entry opens with a navigation map and concise orientation, defines the loop and its limits, describes observed practices, then moves through use cases, evidence status, configuration/evaluation guidance, quick reference, glossary, open questions, and provenance (RESULT, lines 11–19, 21–50, 52–145, 147–182). This progression meets the mini-book criterion without presenting the audits as the reader-facing content.
- **Claim traceability and distinctions — generally supported.** Case claims cite the reviewed audit and E#; the text identifies the relevant task/system/comparator and separates access-to-AI effects from loop-policy effects (for example RESULT, lines 58–62, 68–72, 86–88, and 114–117). Observed practices are explicitly identified as qualitative/descriptive and not prevalence estimates (lines 44–50); configuration sections label bounded evidence, practice-based advice, and inference (lines 121–145).
- **Outcomes, limits, and corrections from S3 — supported.** The RESULT accurately preserves the ICLR trial’s positive selected blind-review result alongside its null average-score-change result and selection caveat (lines 86–88, 110, 115). It also correctly states that the DRV benchmark's tests both guide repairs and define the final score, so its score is not independent of its loop signal, and preserves the test-coverage limitation (lines 102, 137, 143). The reported +36.8% PlowPilot outcome is correctly described as subjective ratings, not task success (line 78).
- **Evidence gaps and scope — supported.** It says no direct user-agent stopping-policy trial jointly testing independent quality and user/resource cost was located, describes thin direct research/analysis efficacy evidence, and bounds this as a search gap rather than proof of absence (lines 115, 168–180). The provenance section identifies the three reviewed audits and states the work is not exhaustive or systematic (lines 174–182).

## Uncertainties

- I reviewed the RESULT against the plan and the reviewed audit records, not by re-extracting each underlying primary study. No additional source-level verification was required for this assignment; the material claims appear traceable to the audited E# records.
