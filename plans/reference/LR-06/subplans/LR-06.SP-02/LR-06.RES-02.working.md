# Working

## Research brief and boundaries

- **Question:** Find concrete cases of people using AI agents in information-work workflows and determine what is known about the associated feedback/iteration patterns and outcomes. The user ultimately needs a mini-book of cases, including when/how loops fit and what has evidence; this unit supplies case-level evidence, not final guidance.
- **Consumer:** S2 case comparison and later S3 assessment of conditions, costs, and failure. Use S1's reviewed map as discovery context, not as a closed taxonomy. LR-05 was not searched or used.
- **Rigor:** Standard academic research. Prioritize primary empirical work and well-documented deployments. Search results and abstracts are leads; where full text is inaccessible, restrict claims to the accessible record and mark the limit.
- **Date:** 2026-10-07 (Europe/Lisbon). Access dates refer to this collection pass.
- **Scope boundary:** Core cases require a person using an AI agent in a work-like information task. Include adjacent conversational AI, code-completion, or scripted collaboration evidence only when it supplies a useful comparison for loop effects; label it adjacent. Exclude model training and agent-only benchmark gains as direct user-workflow evidence.
- **Comparison questions:** Was the loop, its timing, or interaction policy actually manipulated? Could observed differences instead reflect AI access, extra turns, human attention, tool access, or compute? What measured outcome and resource cost moved together?

## Claim map under development

- **C1 / observed workflow cases:** What people do with agents before, during, and after actions, and how this varies across information-work tasks.
- **C2 / effect evidence:** Which loop patterns have measured effects on task quality, correctness, time, completion, user understanding, trust, or other outcomes; record outcome-specific evidence rather than a generic “works.”
- **C3 / loop contribution:** Identify comparisons that isolate loop structure/timing from AI access, extra turns, compute, tools, and human attention; distinguish randomized loop tests from comparisons where the loop is observed or bundled.
- **C4 / practices and conditions:** What people actually do, where they spend attention, how they correct/steer/stop, and which conditions or failure modes are reported.
- **C5 / outcome polarity:** Retain positive, null, mixed, and adverse evidence; record explicitly when no category is found for direct agent-loop outcomes.
- **C6 / transfer:** Identify task, model, setting, sample, outcome, and provenance limits that constrain transfer to user workflow cases.

## Candidate disposition and material discoveries

- **Retained as direct cases:** Pairit ad-creation human-agent RCT and field ad test; Zhou checkpoint schedule experiment and formative trajectories; Kumar developer-agent observational study; Dhanorkar developer oversight interviews; Huq et al. CowCorpus/PlowPilot real web-task trajectories and user study; Pan et al. interactive deep research user study; Wang et al. Alibaba service field experiment (abstract-only access); single-analyst questionnaire analysis case from the Springer paper. These vary in evidential strength and should not be pooled.
- **Retained as adjacent contrasts:** Qian & Wexler's Bard programming exam (conversational assistant, not autonomous agent); Drosos et al.'s researcher-mediated data-analysis workflow (GenAI assistant, not tool-using agent); *Scaffolding Human–AI Collaboration* (Copilot/protocol intervention, not agentic execution); BCG GPT-4 experiment (not loop-specific). Each can challenge overgeneralization but is not counted as direct loop evidence.
- **Set aside from core evidence:** CollabSearch (LLM/RAG rather than agent, no full-text outcome extraction); autonomous-agent pull-request comparison (large deployment signal but does not examine the user's feedback loop); *Completion != Collaboration* (scenario analysis, not empirical deployment outcomes); agent-generated field-experiment variant study (system-side learning rather than user-agent feedback); AgileGen (relevant design claim, but no need to rely on a single-system user satisfaction study when direct loop-isolation and workflow studies better serve the S2 questions).
- **Outcome categories at collection end:** Direct cases contain positive reported effects (Zhou overall time/preference; Pairit quantity/text quality; CowCorpus perceived usefulness; Alibaba technical escalation quality preservation), mixed/heterogeneous effects (Pairit text vs image/diversity; Zhou early/mid/late error location; Alibaba technical vs emotional escalations), and adverse findings (Alibaba lower AI-eligible ratings/emotional escalation; developers' monitoring/review burden; Pairit image quality and diversity tradeoffs). No direct agent-loop study in this bounded collection cleanly reports a null *overall causal loop effect*; Zhou's mid-error benefit is near zero (~2%), and the direct studies with other endpoints do not justify calling their unmeasured loop outcome null. Adjacent evidence includes null/mixed findings (Bard on search questions; Copilot field protocol continuous individual quality null under a ceiling-limited rubric) and adverse results (mandated pair protocol lower quality/production; GPT-4 outside-capability task). These do not substitute for a direct agent-loop null.

## Route coverage and known gaps

- **Covered:** user feedback before/while/after execution; coding and code review; multi-step browser/UI actions; information-seeking/deep research; spreadsheet/data analysis; collaborative creative production; customer service; direct comparative loop/checkpoint studies; human practice and adverse oversight behaviors.
- **Thin or missing:** direct causal comparisons of iterative vs one-shot user feedback in deep research, writing, planning, analysis, and coding-agent tasks; long-run field studies that track loop policy, compute/tool use, outcomes, and user burden together; direct null studies with adequate power and validated task-quality measures; transparent costs for many commercial agent deployments.
- **Stop rationale:** the mapped routes yielded a broad direct case set plus distinct designs (field/controlled comparison, simulated checkpoint manipulation, real-user trajectory corpus, qualitative user studies, and descriptive collaboration records), and a deliberate disconfirmation pass. Further searching is likely to add additional instances, but the key comparison distinction is now clear: one study isolates checkpoint timing; most other cases measure AI collaboration or naturally occurring feedback without isolating a loop's causal contribution. The outcome categories are stated with explicit null gaps rather than being declared complete. This stop is for this bounded case-finding unit only; open-web recall is unknown and systematic citation chaining across all source families remains unperformed.
