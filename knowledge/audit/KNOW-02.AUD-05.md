### Scope limits, assumptions, and exclusions

- This is a targeted follow-up to the reviewed LR-07.RES-01 audit, not a new broad vendor survey. The S1 audit, KNOW-02, and its existing empirical audits are the fixed integration context.
- The working assumption is that S2 will integrate only claims that fit an existing KNOW-02 section or case. A proposed claim that would require a new subject or standalone vendor section is outside scope and should be omitted, not used to widen the research question.
- Targeted first-party searching is warranted only when a material integration claim is not directly covered by S1, when S1 leaves a qualification unresolved, or when a first-party page points to material necessary to state the claim accurately. Record the precise gap before searching.
- Product capability, example, recommendation, vendor-reported outcome, and independent evaluation are separate evidence types. Vendor documentation alone cannot establish an independent effect or that a configuration is generally effective.
- Product/version/date and stated user/workflow context matter because capabilities and advice may be product-specific or change over time. No geographic, population-effect, or comparative-effectiveness question is assigned here.
- Exclude broad effectiveness research, third-party commentary, generic product feature discovery without a clear feedback-loop integration claim, and exhaustive coverage of vendors. Do not repeat the existing empirical synthesis.

### Order and dependencies

1. Map each proposed KNOW-02 addition to one of C1–C5 and to the exact existing section/case it would change.
2. Check the reviewed S1 material against that claim's required elements. Do not search where S1 directly covers the full proposed statement and its needed qualifications.
3. For a material uncovered element, conduct a narrowly targeted first-party search; record the gap, route, and source in the S2 records. If the necessary source cannot be found or accessed, narrow or omit the integration claim.
4. Any effectiveness statement depends on C5's outcome-basis classification; a vendor-reported result must not be used as independent outcome evidence.
