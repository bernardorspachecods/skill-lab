# Audit

## Question

What practical feedback-loop patterns and operating guidance do official first-party vendor sources document for AI agents in user workflows relevant to KNOW-02, and what kind of evidence does each source provide—product capability, vendor recommendation, vendor-reported outcome, or independently tested result?

This is a bounded source audit, not an assessment of whether a vendor's approach is effective. Later phases should use KNOW-02 to determine relevance and integrate only material that fits its existing cases and explanations.

### Phase 4 — findings

- **C1 — documented mechanics and configurations (Supported; moderate confidence).** The bounded corpus documents several distinct workflow loop forms: goal-directed work with an explicit goal exit condition and turn cap (E19), user feedback and acceptance criteria (E10), checks followed by repair or another pass (E3–E6), tool-result feedback into the next model step (E9, E101), evaluator feedback and maker-checker revision (E101, E103), human request/checkpoint/approval or handoff paths (E102, E104, E106), and an example linking traces, feedback, reusable evaluations, and a Codex implementation handoff (E17). E18 documents human review points and developer approval in that cookbook example. These are varied product-specific loop forms in sources from Anthropic, OpenAI, Microsoft, and Google (S1–S16), not interchangeable methods or evidence that loops improve outcomes. Recurring schedules are a separate configuration capability (E7), not by themselves a feedback loop. The ledger's KNOW-02 mappings route this material to existing orientation, cycle, case, supervision, intervention, and configuration sections; those mappings are relevance aids, not independent evidence. A recurring documentation pattern across vendors is not proof of effectiveness.
- **C2 — vendor guidance and stated limits (Supported; moderate confidence).** First-party guidance describes applying patterns selectively (E2), turning repeated checks into configured verification steps (E4), setting an iteration/retry bound and escalation condition (E6, E107–E108, E111), using human checkpoints or approval for actions needing judgment or higher-risk execution (E8, E104, E109–E112), measuring iterative changes against a dataset or grader (E13), and using reviewed configuration with developer approval in the S16 example (E18). These are vendor recommendations or documented controls/examples, not demonstrated best practices. Limits include product-specific controls, probabilistic or imperfect completion/judging, and context, action-risk, and review conditions (E6, E8, E107–E112, E18). Microsoft labels its Agent Framework loop feature experimental and documents a language-availability limit (S9); that qualification must accompany any guidance based on it. The evidence does not establish an optimal retry threshold or universally best configuration.
- **C3 — basis for reported benefits or outcomes (Supported; moderate confidence within this corpus).** The inspected vendor material supplies no independently tested outcome. S6 reports OpenAI's internal estimate that its software product was built in about one-tenth the time manual implementation would have taken (E21). The article supplies no comparison design or independent verification, so this is a vendor-reported internal estimate, not a causal or general performance result. S7 reports 90% precision and recall after about six weeks and substantial engineering oversight, but the account gives no metric-specific method, control, independent verification, or causal attribution for that figure (E12); keep it vendor-reported, not a verified effect estimate. S7 also describes a distinct Tax AI field-completion measure that increased from one quarter of returns at launch to 86% reaching the stated accuracy threshold within six weeks (E20). Keep this vendor-reported measure separate from the rental-property precision/recall result; neither is independently validated here. S8's percentage example is hypothetical (E14), and S3's stale-notebook scenario is intentionally constructed (E15). Other benefit language is vendor assertion, internal practice/status reporting, or a guide's rationale without a described independent evaluation (E16, E113–E117). No effectiveness estimate or causal conclusion follows, and this bounded first-party corpus cannot establish that independent evaluations do not exist.

### Scope limits, assumptions, and exclusions

- Focus on official material published or maintained by vendors of AI-agent products or platforms, prioritizing Claude/Anthropic and OpenAI/ChatGPT/Codex, then other vendors where a first-party source directly fits KNOW-02. The source must be opened and read in-run; search snippets are leads only.
- Treat KNOW-02 as the later integration relevance map. Do not expand the subject beyond its existing cases and explanations, and do not treat generic agent capability pages as relevant without a clear workflow feedback loop.
- Vendor documentation can establish what a vendor documents, supports, demonstrates, or recommends. It cannot by itself establish independent effectiveness. Independent evaluation is outside this first-party source audit unless a vendor page points to a study that later phases separately open and assess.
- No geographic or population comparison is intended. Product versions, publication/update dates, and any stated user or workflow conditions matter because vendor capabilities and recommendations are product-specific and may change.
- Exclude general product marketing without actionable loop details, third-party commentary, and broad academic effectiveness research. These exclusions keep this supplement focused on the missed first-party source role rather than repeating the existing empirical research.
- The audit is a bounded coverage effort, not an exhaustive inventory of every vendor or feature. Record search routes, inaccessible pages, and coverage limits in the assigned working and audit records.
