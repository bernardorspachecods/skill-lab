## Strongest response and why

**D** is strongest. It connects the source concepts to coordinator decisions, keeps user context and acceptance authority with the coordinator, and distinguishes a signal for another cycle from the final outcome check. It also preserves the existing integrity check and names concrete failure modes. Its recommendation for a shared reference is conditional enough to remain proportionate.

## Biggest blind spot

**B** most overstates the case for integration: it assigns moderately high confidence that the pattern will improve clarity and follow-up quality, although the brief supplies no outcome evidence for that claim. Its detailed handoff fields could also become a default contract for every delegation despite the stated concern about added overhead.

## What all responses missed

All five assume the coordinator can judge the returned artifact and evidence. They do not explain what to do when the coordinator lacks the expertise or access needed to verify correctness. A useful pattern should make that uncertainty actionable—such as seeking a qualified review or escalating to the user—instead of treating more agent-provided evidence as sufficient.
