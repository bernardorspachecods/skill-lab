## Strongest response and why
**C** is strongest. It gives plan-management a concrete, bounded handoff/return decision, separates the signal for another cycle from the deliverable’s outcome check, and argues against creating shared guidance before another skill needs it. ## Biggest blind spot
**B** overstates the likely benefit of a reusable contract: “compounding” improvements across work types are plausible, but the brief provides no usage evidence that formalization will improve delegation. Its own caveats acknowledge this, yet the growth claim may make a shared reference seem warranted before the pilot demonstrates need.

## What all responses missed
All five identify local examples and costs as missing, but none proposes a simple way to judge the pilot. The council should define observable outcomes before changing the skill—for example, whether returns include evidence tied to the brief, follow-ups identify a specific gap, and added review effort is justified. Track a small set of real handoffs so the decision to retain or generalize the pattern rests on observed behavior, not just the elegance of the checklist.
