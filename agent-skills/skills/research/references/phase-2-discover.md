# Phase 2: Plan and discover

**Role.** You build the map of where evidence should exist, then collect a body of plausible in-scope sources from it. You are the collector, not the judge. The evaluator in phase 3 decides what the material is worth and doesn't see your impressions, so keep verdicts out.

Read `shared.md` first if you haven't.

## Inputs

The claim matrix (claims, importance, evidence expectations, scope limits), the level, a search ceiling per claim, and any sources the user supplied.

## What to produce

1. **A source map**: for each material claim, the source roles, producers, sites, and domain terms where evidence should exist (official records, registries, standards bodies, the original study or dataset, trade or academic venues, practitioner sources). Revise it as you learn.
2. **Collected sources** in the source ledger (the fields you own, see `shared.md`), each opened or inspected, with its access mode. Include user-supplied sources, marked as such.
3. **`Q#` search records** in the working record. Use one record per submitted query or citation lead followed, and list every `C#` the action was intended to serve. A shared action counts once toward each listed claim's ceiling. Record separate searches separately, even when they follow the same route or serve overlapping claims.
4. **A coverage summary** in the audit record (format in `shared.md`).

The output is a body of collected material, not a conclusion. Don't state or hint at answers, don't rank sources by quality, and don't record whether a source supports a claim. Record only what you need to find it again and what role it appears to play.

## Method

Plan from the claims. Start broad and narrow later. Don't judge sufficiency until the planned routes have been worked across all material claims, and don't claim a measured recall you can't measure.

Use these passes as the scope requires:

1. **Orient broadly** to find the terms, producers, and source types for the topic.
2. **Hunt by source role**: official sites and records, registries, standards, original studies and datasets, trade or academic venues, practitioner sources.
3. **Chase citations**: follow backward from sources to what they cite, and forward to what cites them.
4. **Search for disconfirmation**: deliberately look for sources that conflict with, qualify, correct, or retract what the others suggest. This pass is not optional at Standard or High-stakes.

While collecting:

- Open and keep plausible in-scope candidates, including ones that don't confirm what you expect. Set aside only obvious noise and clearly out-of-scope material.
- Defer comparative quality judgments and inclusion decisions to phase 3.
- Spread effort across the mapped roles and producers. Don't let one productive site absorb the search.
- Treat snippets, rankings, and your own recall as leads. Open the source.
- Where useful, use a few known relevant documents as sentinels: check that collection finds them, and widen it if it doesn't. Record the result, or that no sentinel was available.

## Search records

Each `Q#` record holds one submitted query or one citation lead followed, the `C#` claims it serves, date, purpose, surface searched, candidates collected or inspected, terms added or rejected and why, and what the action contributed to scope coverage. Use the records to see which claims, source roles, and routes still need coverage. A low-yield query, or one that returns nothing new, is not by itself a reason to stop.

The records are disposable. Don't copy rejected leads into the audit record. Promote only material provenance, plus the concise coverage summary and stop rationale.

## When to stop

The brief gives a search ceiling per claim. It is an upper bound, not a target.

- **Below the ceiling**, stop when the source map has been worked across all material claims, the citation paths have been followed, and no known in-scope route is likely to add a materially different body of evidence. Then ask whether the collection covers the scope and the importance of each claim, and search the gaps if not. Search harder where evidence is scarce or consequences are high.
- **At the ceiling** for a claim, do not search that claim further until the orchestrator decides whether to extend its budget. If a mapped route could materially change the answer, send a reopen request targeting phase 2; name the claim, route, expected contribution, and proposed revised ceiling. Continue searching other claims within their ceilings while you wait. If the orchestrator accepts, resume up to the revised ceiling. If no route is likely to matter or the request is declined, record the remaining routes and mark that claim's coverage as budget-constrained in the coverage summary and `Limits:`. Do not present it as fully covered.

## Reopen requests

Send one to phase 1 if a claim turns out ill-posed or unanswerable as written, or if a needed subquestion is missing. Don't rewrite the claim set yourself.

## Done when

Every material claim has coverage across the mapped source roles, or a recorded reason it doesn't. The coverage summary and stop rationale are written. Finish with the phase report from `shared.md`.
