# Academic extension for phase 4: literature synthesis, questions, and critique

Read this after `shared.md` and `phase-4-synthesize.md`, when your brief names it. It adds academic stages to synthesis. Run only the stages your brief asks for.

The functions here are the *literature synthesizer* and the *question critic*. Your brief follows the contract in `shared.md`. Don't invent a persona or fill missing fields with plausible content.

## Inputs you must have

You need the `E#` entries as well as the `F#` cards and orientation maps. `F#` cards orient and compare studies. They are not evidence, so a material claim can only rest on the linked `E#` entries. If you were given `F#` cards without `E#` entries, say so and send a reopen request to phase 3 instead of synthesizing from the cards.

```text
paper/source -> orientation map -> finding cards -> thematic synthesis -> question cards
                                        \-> limitations / open questions
```

## 3. Thematic literature synthesis

Organize a literature review by the concepts, mechanisms, methods, populations, or tensions that answer the brief, not as one paragraph per paper. Use the `F#` cards to compare results. Check whether differences in design, sample, measurement, or context explain apparent conflicts.

Separate a gap supported by the reviewed literature from a topic that wasn't searched or was inaccessible.

Don't impose a fixed number of studies, a publication cutoff, or a claim of consensus unless the brief or an appropriate review protocol justifies it. Treat papers from the same dataset, trial, or result as one provenance cluster.

## 4. Research-question cards

When question design is part of the task, generate candidates from supported findings, explicit limitations, and verified gaps. Each card contains:

- the question, narrow enough to be answerable;
- the `F#` finding or limitation and the linked `E#` evidence that motivates it;
- constructs, population, context, and time period;
- a plausible method and data requirement, if requested;
- what would count as an informative answer or disconfirmation;
- feasibility risks and competing explanations;
- a note that novelty or impact is provisional until checked against the relevant literature and constraints.

Questions are proposals, not findings. Don't treat a question generated from an unsupported summary as evidence.

**Novelty and gap checks.** If a card's novelty or gap claim needs a literature check nobody has run, send a reopen request to phase 2 describing the specific check, and keep novelty marked provisional until the result returns. Don't try to settle it from memory.

## 5. Thesis-oriented critique

When the user asks for thesis mentoring, give a bounded diagnosis that distinguishes:

- a gap supported by the reviewed literature;
- a gap suggested by a limitation or context boundary;
- a gap that is only a hypothesis for further searching;
- methodological, theoretical, data, and practical contributions.

Formulate a testable hypothesis only when its constructs, direction (if appropriate), population, and observable measures are explicit enough to test.

If the thesis discipline, research paradigm, ethics process, or institutional requirements could materially change the recommendation, don't guess. Put one focused question under `Clarification needed:` in your phase report so the orchestrator can ask the user.

## Academic deliverables

Include only the stages and outputs the task needs. A single-paper task may need an orientation map and `F#` cards, with open questions when requested or useful. A literature review may need thematic synthesis. A research-question task may need question cards. Thesis mentoring may need a bounded gap and contribution diagnosis. Store stage material in the working or audit record as appropriate. Don't duplicate the claim matrix, `E#` records, source ledger, search records, or stop rationale here; the shared records govern evidence requirements, retention, and final presentation.
