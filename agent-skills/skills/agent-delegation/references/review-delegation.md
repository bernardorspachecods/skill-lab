# Conducting a delegated review

Use this reference when an agent is assigned to assess completed work or a
stable target. Follow the assignment for scope, criteria, context, and requested
output. If any of these are missing or unclear, ask the coordinator to resolve
the gap before reviewing.

## Confirm the review boundary

Start only after the executor declares the assigned work complete and the
coordinator has received and reconciled the result. Confirm which targets are
in scope and which materials are context. Supporting materials help interpret
a target, but are not themselves review targets unless the assignment says so.
Keep the targets stable while reviewing.

Assess the work and its outputs against the assigned criteria. Do not assess
an individual agent's performance, silently expand the scope, or introduce
new criteria as if they were part of the assignment. If a relevant concern
falls outside the criteria, identify it separately for the coordinator.

Reviewers do not change the target or implement recommendations. If the work
needs correction, report the issue and return it to execution. Review fixes
only after the executor completes them and the coordinator reconciles the
updated result. Continue the existing review or begin a new one as directed by
the coordinator.

## Evaluate and support conclusions

Work through the criteria, inspect the relevant parts of each target, and
compare what is present with what the assignment requires. Ground conclusions
in checkable evidence, such as a file and location, a quoted passage, an
observed behavior, or a reproducible result. Explain how the evidence supports
the conclusion; avoid unsupported impressions.

Separate confirmed findings from uncertainties and recommendations. If the
available evidence cannot settle a point, say what is missing and how that
limits the conclusion. Do not present an unverified concern as a confirmed
defect.

If a criterion is outside your expertise, identify that limit and ask the
coordinator to assign a qualified reviewer for that part. Do not imply that
you assessed a criterion you could not competently evaluate.

## Return a useful assessment

Organize the assessment around the assigned criteria or requested format. For
each finding, state the issue, its evidence, and why it matters. Include
material uncertainties, recommendations, and any criterion that could not be
assessed. Make clear whether the finding calls for a correction, is optional
advice, or is informational. Use priority or severity labels when they help
the coordinator act on the finding and fit the assignment. Keep the response
focused on the assignment and return only the output requested by the
coordinator.

The coordinator checks that the assessment covers the targets and criteria,
that its conclusions are supported, and that uncertainties or disagreements
are preserved. With multiple reviewers, the coordinator integrates their
assigned perspectives into the final assessment. With one reviewer, the
reviewer may prepare the final assessment directly; the coordinator still
checks the handoff.

## Use additional reviewers selectively

Use one reviewer by default. Add reviewers only when independent perspectives
or a distinct specialist view would materially improve the assessment. Give
each reviewer a distinct question or angle and keep their initial assessments
independent: reviewers must not read one another's conclusions before
submitting their own. Each reviewer returns only their assigned output and
must not edit another reviewer's work or the coordinator's final assessment.

If delegated review is unavailable, say so explicitly. Do not present the
primary agent's own assessment as an independent review.
