# Redo plan protocol

Use this protocol when a user says that a plan, research effort, or its result
missed the need they had, or asks to redo the work. The purpose is to recover
the intended outcome with the smallest adequate change to the plan. A redo is
not automatically a full restart, and the user is not presumed to be at fault
for a mismatch between their need and the plan's stated objective.

**The user's clarified need determines the intended outcome. The durable plan
contract determines plan structure and execution gates.** Apply higher-priority
instructions first. For user direction, apply these rules:

- A newer, explicit clarification replaces an older plan assumption or
  objective where they conflict.
- Keep earlier explicit boundaries in force until the user explicitly changes
  them.
- Ask the user when the intended change to an objective, boundary, or
  execution gate remains unclear.

The coordinator owns the diagnosis and revised plan with the user. It does not
repeat assigned research, implementation, or formal review itself. Follow the
[durable plan contract](durable-plan-contract.md) for artifact structure,
assignment, review, and execution gates whenever the redo changes a durable
plan.

## 1. Pause and re-anchor on the need

Do not defend the prior result, close the plan, or start repeating work. Briefly
state the mismatch as understood, using the user's correction where possible.
Separate:

- what the user wanted to learn, decide, make, or do;
- what the agreed plan promised to deliver;
- what the work actually produced; and
- which constraints or assumptions shaped that result.

Do not equate compliance with the plan with usefulness to the user. A result
can be well-supported and still answer the wrong question. Conversely, user
dissatisfaction alone does not show the research or implementation was poor.

Ask one clarifying question only when a remaining ambiguity would materially
change the revised objective or work. Otherwise state a reasonable assumption
and continue the diagnosis.

## 2. Locate the mismatch

Classify the main cause or causes. These categories are diagnostic prompts, not
mutually exclusive labels:

- **Intent or objective:** the plan optimized for a different decision or
  outcome than the user needed.
- **Scope or audience:** the work covered the wrong cases, users, setting, or
  level of practical detail.
- **Evidence or execution:** relevant evidence, implementation, or verification
  is missing, weak, stale, or improperly assessed.
- **Synthesis or presentation:** useful evidence exists but is not organized
  into an answer the user can apply.
- **Constraint or process:** a plan boundary, checkpoint, or workflow choice
  prevented the intended deliverable from being produced.

Name the evidence for the diagnosis and distinguish observed facts from
inferences. Do not label a missed need as a user-communication failure when the
plan's own objective or completion criteria made the miss likely.

## 3. Audit prior work for reuse

Review the completed outputs against the revised need. For each material
artifact or finding, decide whether it is:

- **Reusable:** valid for the revised scope and sufficiently detailed for the
  intended use.
- **Reusable with limits:** relevant, but its evidence, population, outcome, or
  transfer boundary must remain visible.
- **Needs a targeted supplement:** useful, with a specific unanswered question
  or missing detail.
- **Superseded or unusable:** based on a premise or scope that no longer
  supports the intended outcome, or not reliable enough to carry forward.

Preserve reviewed work and its provenance. Do not repeat valid work merely to
make the redo look comprehensive. Do not carry findings into the new synthesis
without their evidence strength, conflicts, and limits. Mark superseded
conclusions clearly instead of silently overwriting their history.

## 4. Select the smallest adequate redo

Choose the scope that repairs the diagnosed cause:

| Redo scope | Use when |
| --- | --- |
| Reframe or rewrite the synthesis | The needed evidence is already present, but the plan asked the wrong question or the result hid the useful answer. |
| Targeted evidence supplement | Existing work is relevant, but a defined gap prevents a sound or useful answer. |
| Rerun one or more phases | The affected phases have material coverage, method, or evidence-quality problems that a supplement cannot repair. |
| Restart the plan | The underlying framing, inputs, or method invalidates most prior work, or the user explicitly wants a fresh start. |

State why the selected scope is sufficient and what it leaves unresolved. When
reasonable alternatives would produce materially different results, show the
tradeoffs and recommend one.

## 5. Revise the plan contract

Before execution, make the revised plan concrete and reviewable. It should
state:

- the intended user-facing outcome and audience;
- the practical questions the work must answer;
- the boundaries and assumptions;
- which prior outputs remain inputs, which are superseded, and what new work is
  needed;
- the deliverable shape and how claims, evidence, uncertainty, and
  recommendations will be distinguished; and
- completion criteria that test whether the intended need was met, as well as
  whether the work was carried out rigorously.

Make the success criteria answer-shaped. For example, if the user needs to
choose when and how to use a technique, require usable cases, fit conditions,
an evidence-backed procedure, observed outcomes, and known limitations. Do not
allow a neighboring conclusion, such as whether to change one local workflow,
to substitute for that deliverable.

**Illustrative example:** A user wanted practical cases for using loops in
AI-agent workflows, but the plan ended with a recommendation about changing
repository skills. Keep prior mechanism and application evidence where it
supports the new goal, identify which agent-workflow cases lack evidence about
when and how to use them, and revise the deliverable into a case guide. The
repository recommendation does not satisfy that guide unless the user asks for
it as an additional outcome. Apply the same reasoning to other mismatches:
preserve relevant evidence, research only material gaps, and shape the output
around the clarified need.

For durable plans, update the canonical plan and artifacts according to the
durable plan contract. Preserve prior research and review records and their
provenance. The durable plan contract remains authoritative for plan structure,
artifact identities, validation, and execution gates.

## 6. Checkpoint, execute, and close against the need

Present the diagnosis, reuse audit, selected redo scope, revised outcome, and
material tradeoffs to the user. Ask the user to agree to a revised objective,
scope, output, and execution contract before starting newly scoped work. For a
durable plan, follow its approved execution gates when resuming work.

After execution, check both dimensions:

1. **Work quality:** evidence, implementation, review, and plan gates meet the
   revised criteria.
2. **Need fit:** the deliverable answers the user's practical questions in a
   form they can use.

Report any remaining gap plainly. Do not mark a plan complete solely because
its original criteria passed if the agreed redo criteria remain unmet. The
agreed redo criteria govern completion for the revised work.

## Application record

When the redo is substantial, keep a concise record with the plan describing
the user's clarified need, mismatch diagnosis, reuse decisions, selected redo
scope, revised completion criteria, and checkpoint outcome. Keep the record
with the canonical plan rather than creating a parallel tracker.
