# Redo plan protocol

Use this protocol when the user says a plan, research effort, or result missed
their need, or asks to redo the work. Diagnose the mismatch and make the
smallest adequate change; a redo does not imply a full restart.

The user's clarified need determines the intended outcome. The
[durable plan contract](durable-plan-contract.md) determines durable-plan
structure and execution gates. Apply higher-priority instructions first. For
conflicting user directions:

- A newer, explicit clarification replaces an older objective or assumption.
- Earlier explicit boundaries remain in force until the user changes them.
- Ask when the intended change to the objective, boundary, or execution gate
  remains unclear.

The coordinator diagnoses the mismatch and revises the plan with the user. It
does not redo assigned research, implementation, or formal review itself. For
durable plans, follow the durable plan contract for artifacts, assignments,
reviews, and execution gates.

## 1. Re-anchor on the need

Pause new execution while diagnosing. Do not defend the prior result, close the
plan, or repeat work before choosing a redo scope.

Briefly state the mismatch as understood, using the user's correction where
possible. Separate:

- what the user wanted to learn, decide, make, or do;
- what the agreed plan promised;
- what the work produced; and
- which constraints or assumptions shaped it.

Do not equate plan compliance with usefulness: sound work can answer the wrong
question, while dissatisfaction alone does not show that the work was poor.
Ask a clarifying question only if the ambiguity would materially change the
revised objective or work. Otherwise state a reasonable assumption and
continue.

## 2. Diagnose the mismatch

Use these causes as prompts; more than one may apply:

- **Intent or objective:** the plan aimed at a different decision or outcome.
- **Scope or audience:** it covered the wrong cases, users, setting, or level
  of practical detail.
- **Evidence or execution:** relevant evidence, implementation, or verification
  is missing, weak, stale, or improperly assessed.
- **Synthesis or presentation:** useful evidence exists but is not organized
  into an answer the user can apply.
- **Constraint or process:** a boundary, checkpoint, or workflow choice
  prevented the intended deliverable.

Name the evidence for the diagnosis and distinguish observed facts from
inferences. Do not blame a communication failure when the plan's objective or
completion criteria made the mismatch likely.

## 3. Audit prior work

Review completed outputs against the clarified need. Classify each material
artifact or finding:

- **Reusable:** valid for the revised scope and sufficiently detailed.
- **Reusable with limits:** relevant, but its evidence, population, outcome,
  or transfer boundary must remain visible.
- **Needs a targeted supplement:** useful, with a specific unanswered question
  or missing detail.
- **Superseded or unusable:** its premise or scope no longer supports the need,
  or it is not reliable enough to carry forward.

Preserve reviewed work and provenance. Do not repeat valid work for the sake of
completeness, and retain evidence strength, conflicts, and limits when using
findings in a new synthesis. Mark superseded conclusions instead of silently
overwriting their history.

## 4. Choose the smallest adequate redo

| Scope | Choose when |
| --- | --- |
| Reframe or rewrite the synthesis | Needed evidence is present, but the plan asked the wrong question or the result obscured the useful answer. |
| Targeted evidence supplement | Existing work is relevant, but a defined gap prevents a sound or useful answer. |
| Rerun one or more phases | Affected phases have material coverage, method, or evidence-quality problems that a supplement cannot repair. |
| Restart the plan | The framing, inputs, or method invalidate most prior work, or the user explicitly wants a fresh start. |

Explain why the chosen scope is sufficient and what it leaves unresolved. If
reasonable alternatives would produce materially different results, show the
tradeoffs and recommend one.

## 5. Revise the plan contract

Before execution, make the revised plan concrete and reviewable. Specify:

- the intended user-facing outcome and audience;
- the practical questions to answer;
- boundaries and assumptions;
- prior outputs to reuse, supersede, or supplement, and the new work required;
- deliverable shape, including how claims, evidence, uncertainty, and
  recommendations will be distinguished; and
- completion criteria that test both need fit and rigor.

Make success criteria answer-shaped. If the user needs to choose when and how
to use a technique, for example, require usable cases, fit conditions, an
evidence-backed procedure, observed outcomes, and known limitations. A
neighboring recommendation does not satisfy that deliverable unless the user
also asks for it.

For durable plans, update the canonical plan and artifacts under the durable
plan contract. Preserve prior research and review records with their
provenance.

## 6. Agree, execute, and close against the need

Present the diagnosis, reuse audit, selected scope, revised outcome, and
material tradeoffs. Get the user's agreement to the revised objective, scope,
output, and execution contract before starting newly scoped work. For a
durable plan, resume under its approved execution gates.

After execution, check both:

1. **Work quality:** evidence, implementation, review, and plan gates meet the
   revised criteria.
2. **Need fit:** the deliverable answers the user's practical questions in a
   usable form.

Report remaining gaps plainly. The plan is complete only when the agreed redo
criteria are met, even if the original criteria passed.

For a substantial redo, keep a concise record with the canonical plan: the
clarified need, diagnosis, reuse decisions, selected scope, revised completion
criteria, and checkpoint outcome. Do not create a parallel tracker.
